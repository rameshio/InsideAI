## A fluent answer can still be wrong
AI hallucination is a common name for output that is incorrect or unsupported but presented as if it were reliable. An assistant might invent a citation, confuse two people, or provide an outdated instruction.

## Why this happens
Language models generate plausible continuations. They do not automatically check every sentence against a trusted database. Training gaps, ambiguous prompts, and missing context can all contribute to mistakes. Even a system with search or retrieval can misread its sources.

## A simple verification loop
1. Separate claims from suggestions.
2. Open the primary source for each important claim.
3. Confirm dates, scope, and qualifications.
4. Reproduce technical instructions in a safe test environment.
5. Remove claims you cannot verify.

<Callout>Asking a model to check its own answer can help identify issues, but it is not independent verification.</Callout>

## When to slow down
Claims about prices, benchmarks, policies, and medical or legal decisions deserve extra scrutiny. A reassuring tone does not reduce the consequences of a mistake.
