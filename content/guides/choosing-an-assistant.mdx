## Start with your task
An assistant that works well for brainstorming may be a poor fit for private document analysis. Decide what success looks like before comparing products.

| Task | What to look for | How to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learning a concept | Clear explanations and adjustable depth | Ask the same question at two levels |
| Coding | Useful tests and explanations | Run the code and inspect failures |
| Document research | Accurate citations and access controls | Check answers against passages |
| Creative drafting | Consistency with your brief | Compare drafts with a written rubric |

## Build a small evaluation
Choose three representative tasks. Run each with the same instructions and source material. Score correctness, clarity, editing time, and whether you can verify the output. Repeat a task when reliability matters.

## Check the details before committing
Review current terms, data controls, accessibility, and actual costs on the provider’s official site. This guide deliberately avoids price or feature claims that change frequently.

## Keep a record
Save the prompt, output, date, and your observations. A lightweight evaluation journal is more useful than a leaderboard that does not reflect your work.
